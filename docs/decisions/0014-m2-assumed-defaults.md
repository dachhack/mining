# 0014 — M2 assumed defaults and rule readings

Date: 2026-11-18. Status: assumed by the coordinator under agent plan section 6, except where an item quotes the owner. Every assumed item is a reading of a [Default] or of a point the design doc leaves open, and the owner can change any of them. No [Decided] item is changed. Section 8 (camps) is [Decided]; items 26–31 fill in what it leaves open and do not alter it.

## Context

Milestone M2 (hazards and camps) needs answers to owner questions 4 and 8–13, and a concrete form for two answers the owner gave on 2026-11-18 after playtest round m1-r2: "Straight shafts should have cave in risk" (question 52) and "Start with fuel to get to coal so we don't need to have close coal necessary" (question 53). So that every developer builds on the same reading, the coordinator fixed one here. Every number below lives in `src/data`. Section numbers refer to `docs/game-design-doc.md`.

A developer who finds that an item here cannot work as written says so in the task's PR and proposes the smallest change; the coordinator updates this note. Words used throughout: a _step_ is a move to a face neighbour; _beside_ means a face neighbour; distances are counted in steps through the grid (Manhattan) unless an item says "through open cells".

## Decision

### The cube phase and what the player knows

1. **Order.** `endShift` runs the cube phase and then upkeep, as design doc 4 says. The cube phase, in this fixed order:
   - (a) Support check and falling blocks (items 5–10): supports with no hold are destroyed (item 6); then blocks whose timer is 0 and whose countdown is running fall, from the bottom layer up; then newly unsupported blocks crack.
   - (b) Fluids flow: water (item 12), then lava (item 16), then water beside lava turns to stone.
   - (c) Gas spreads (item 13); then vents act; then armed unstable ore and placed explosives go off (items 15, 17); then ignition (item 14), repeated until nothing new explodes.
   - (d) Crack timers tick: every crack timer whose countdown is running goes down by 1, including one set in this phase.
   - (e) Effects on the avatar (item 2), then the death check.

   Then upkeep (fuel, camp fuel, lamp oil; out of fuel), then the forecast for the next shift. Nothing in the cube phase uses the RNG.

2. **The avatar during the cube phase.** The avatar does not move during (a)–(d). Damage is recorded when its event happens and applied in (e) in that order; the first cause that brings health to 0 or below names the death, and health is checked before air. Rest (item 27) is applied only if the avatar is alive after damage and air. Then `settle` runs once (an uncontrolled fall, decision note 0010), then the death check. Death is at health 0 or below for every cause. Structures and camps are destroyed at the moment of the event that destroys them, so a torch destroyed by a rockfall in (a) is not there to light gas in (c); step (e) applies effects to the avatar only.
3. **What the player sees.** The player sees the current contents (water, gas, lava, cracks, timers, structures, loose items) of every revealed cell, lit or not. The contents of unrevealed cells are never shown, and no legal action or its cost depends on them.
4. **Forecast.** `previewCubePhase` is a function of what item 3 lets the player see and nothing else. It has two parts. (1) Countdowns: every revealed block whose countdown is running and shows 1, every armed unstable ore, and every placed explosive is forecast, with a falling block's path reported down to the first unrevealed cell and no further. (2) Flows: steps (b) and (c) are run on a copy of the cube in which every unrevealed cell is plain rock with nothing in it, and the moves that result are forecast. So the forecast says what the miner can work out: a flow can turn out differently when something unseen takes part; a countdown cannot. New cracks are not forecast. The property test of decision note 0011 is extended: changing any unrevealed cell must not change the forecast or the action list.

### Cave-ins (6.1, question 8)

5. **Supported.** A block is supported if (a) the cell below it is solid, is bedrock, or holds water or lava, or (b) there is a path of at most its span in steps on its own layer, every cell on it solid, from the block to a block supported by (a), or (c) a placed support holds it. Span does not chain: a block held only by span does not lend span to others. Span is a number for each block in `src/data/blocks.ts`: dirt and rubble 0, hard rock 3, every other block 2. Rule (a) is local: a block resting on a cracked block counts as supported until that one falls, so collapses cascade slowly; that is intended. Water and lava hold up what is over them and gas does not, so a block over a pocket rests on it, and pumping a pocket dry can bring its roof down.
6. **Supports.** A support is placed (1 AP, 1 support) in the avatar's own cell or an open face neighbour on its level. It holds the block directly above it and the four blocks beside that one (a plus shape). It is a structure, and a cell holds one structure; a support is also footing and can be climbed like a ladder, so a supported shaft cell needs no ladder. Everywhere a rule speaks of a ladder as footing or as something to climb (mining the block underfoot in note 0010, the cells a move is judged by in note 0011, the search in item 23), a support counts as one. A support stays only while its cell has a solid cell below it or at least two solid side neighbours, the bedrock beyond the grid counting as solid; otherwise it is destroyed at the start of step (a), before blocks fall, so that what it held cracks in the same step.
7. **Cracking and falling.** A block found unsupported in step (a) of shift N cracks with timer 2. Step (d) lowers it in the same phase, so the countdown shown is 2 during shift N+1 and 1 during shift N+2, and the block falls in step (a) at the end of shift N+2. A cracked block's countdown _runs_ while the block is unsupported by item 5, or is beside a cell of an unlined run that is over its limit (item 10); otherwise it is _stopped_ and the crack stays. This is worked out on the true cube whenever it is read (so after every action, at the start of step (a), and at the start of a shift), never stored, and the player sees which it is. A block falls in step (a) only if its timer is 0 and its countdown is running; step (d) lowers running timers only; a stopped countdown that runs again goes on from where it stopped. So a support placed in the last shift saves the block. The number shown is the stored timer plus 1 (`inShifts` in the forecast): a stored 0 shows as 1 and means the block falls in the coming cube phase.
8. **A falling block** falls through open cells whatever they hold and stops on the first solid cell or bedrock, where it becomes rubble (1 AP to clear, no yield); fluid, gas, and loose items in the cell it comes to rest in are destroyed. It deals 4 damage to an avatar in any cell it passes through or would rest in, and destroys every structure other than a support in those cells. Two exceptions: a support in its path stops it (the block stops in the cell above the support and breaks up there, leaving no rubble; the support's own cell is not entered, an avatar in it is not hurt, and the support is destroyed); and a block that would come to rest in the avatar's own cell deals its damage and breaks up, leaving no rubble there. The avatar is never inside a solid cell.
9. **Mining overhead (question 4).** Legal only when a support is in the avatar's own cell.
10. **Straight shafts (question 52; owner: "Straight shafts should have cave in risk").** 6.1 alone never makes a shaft dangerous, because a shaft's walls rest on the rock below them. Added rule, in the spirit of 6.1:
    - An open cell inside the grid is _tight_ when at least three of its four side neighbours are solid; the bedrock beyond the grid counts as solid.
    - A support _lines_ its own cell and the 2 cells above and below it.
    - An _unlined run_ is a maximal vertical run of tight cells none of which is lined.
    - A run's limit is that of the weakest block among the side neighbours of its cells: dirt and rubble 3, hard rock 8, every other block 5; bedrock is ignored.
    - In step (a), for each unlined run longer than its limit that has no wall block with a running countdown: the side neighbour of the run's top cell with the weakest material, lowest index first, cracks with timer 2 (item 7). If the run stops being too long (a support is placed, or the foot fills with rubble), the countdown stops by item 7.
    - When the block falls it moves into the tight cell beside it (the lowest index if there are two) and falls from there by item 8.

    Ladders do not line a shaft. A one-wide stair is safe from this rule because its vertical runs are 2 or 3 cells; a shaft two cells by two is safe because none of its cells is tight; a shaft one cell by two is not. The rule applies to every open cell, dug or generated, revealed or not.

11. **Dirt roofs.** By item 5 dirt has span 0, as 6.1 says, so the dirt roof of any tunnel or stair is unsupported and falls unless a support holds it. In the upper band a stair under dirt sheds its roof block by block until it reaches daylight or stone. That is the design doc's own rule and is kept; the staircase sim policy and its golden run change. A camp founded under a dirt roof loses it the same way and is destroyed by the first block that falls (item 30); under stone the roof holds.

### Water, gas, lava, unstable ore (6.2–6.5, questions 10, 11)

12. **Water.** A water cell holds one unit. The cells beyond the grid are solid for every flow. Each unit moves at most once a step; cells are taken from the bottom layer up, index order within a layer; a move into a cell already taken this step is skipped.
    - _Fall,_ decided from the state at the start of the step: a unit with an open, dry cell below it moves down one cell.
    - _Level,_ decided from the state after Fall: for each connected body of water (face-connected; bodies taken in the order of their lowest-index cell), each dry open cell that is a face neighbour of a unit of the body that has not moved this step, and is lower than the body's highest unmoved unit, receives one unmoved unit taken from the body's highest cell (last index among the highest), lowest cell first then index order, while such a unit remains.

    So water advances one cell a shift, down a hole or along a tunnel, the cell a unit fell from is refilled from the top of the body, hollows fill from the bottom, and a single layer of water on a flat floor stays where it is. Flow conserves water; rubble, sealant, and stone made with lava can destroy it. A flooded cell costs +1 AP to enter and drains air. A pump removes up to 3 units a shift from water cells within 2 steps of it through open cells, nearest first, then index order. Sealant (1 AP, 1 sealant) turns one open face neighbour that holds no structure into a solid "sealed" block (2 AP to mine, no yield, span 2); fluid, gas, and loose items in it are destroyed.

13. **Gas.** A pocket cell holds 4 units. Each shift a gas cell with u units and n open face neighbours that are neither flooded nor lava gives floor(u / (n + 1)) units to each and keeps the rest; all gifts are computed from the state at the start of the step. A cell with 1 or more units is gassy and drains air. Gas that reaches the surface layer is gone. A cell with fewer units than it has neighbours plus one gives none away, so thin gas stays for the rest of the run unless it is vented or burnt; that is intended. A vent acts after gas spreads and before ignition: it removes all gas within 2 steps of it through open cells, if a path of open cells (gassy or not, not flooded) joins it to the surface.
14. **Ignition (question 10).** The avatar's own lamp does not ignite gas. A torch, lava, or an explosion in or beside a gassy cell ignites it: all the gassy cells connected to it go off as one explosion (item 15). A safety lamp is a placed light, like a torch, that does not ignite.
15. **Explosions.** One explosion deals 6 damage, once, to an avatar in or beside any exploding cell; destroys structures in and beside those cells; and removes the gas. Blocks left unsupported by it crack in the usual way in step (a) of the next phase. An explosive item (placed in an open face neighbour or the avatar's own cell, 1 AP) goes off in step (c) of the cube phase that ends the shift it was placed in: it explodes its own cell and also removes the solid blocks beside it, with no yield. "Radius 1" in the design doc is read everywhere as the cell and its six face neighbours.
16. **Lava.** A lava cell holds one unit and flows by the rules of item 12, but only in the cube phase of even-numbered shifts. Generation marks every cell of a lava pocket larger than 8 cells as a source: a source cell that gives up its unit is full again at once and can give again in the same step, so in one lava step a source fills every cell the Level rule lets it reach; lava from a source ends up in everything open that is connected to it below the top layer of its pocket, and never rises. Lava in the avatar's cell deals 5 damage a shift, lava beside it 1. Lava destroys ladders, torches, supports, vents, pumps, safety lamps, loose items, and camp cells it enters. Water and lava never share a cell; at the end of step (b) every water cell beside a lava cell, and that lava cell, become stone (a source that turns to stone stops being one), except that the avatar's own cell does not turn to stone.
17. **Unstable ore.** Braced when a support stands in a cell beside it. If the avatar mines a block beside it while it is revealed and unbraced, it is armed and goes off in step (c) of the cube phase that ends that shift: the ore block is destroyed with no yield and its cell explodes (item 15); no other block is removed. Only the avatar's mining arms it, and only if the ore was already revealed when the neighbour was mined, so there is never a detonation the player could not have read beforehand (question 58). Mined directly, armed or not, it yields as the block table says and does not detonate; a support placed after it is armed does not disarm it.
18. **Tells (6.2–6.5).** Worked out from where the hazard is now and never stored, as decision note 0012 does for traces. Shown on revealed blocks, lit or not, because they are things a miner can feel: damp stone on blocks within 1 of water, pitted rock within 1 of gas, warm glow within 2 of lava, hairline glow on unstable ore itself, cracks on cracked blocks. (Mineral traces keep their own rule: lit only.)
19. **Generation.** Cube generation adds caverns, water pockets, gas pockets, lava pockets, and unstable ore in the bands design doc 3.1 and 3.3 give, with sizes and densities in `src/data/generation.ts`. Pockets are sealed: every face of a pocket cell touches rock or another cell of the same pocket. Generation makes a fresh cube that is _at rest_: no block is unsupported, no unlined run is over its limit, and no water or lava can move; gas shifting inside a sealed pocket is not counted. To get there it makes no open or gas-filled span wider than its roof's rock can hold (stone 5 cells, hard rock 7, none under dirt) and no tight vertical run longer than its limit. As a safety net the cube is then settled before the run starts (step (a) repeated, every crack falling at once, until nothing is unsupported); a test over many seeds checks that settling moves no block. So nothing falls or flows until the player digs.

### The avatar (5.1, 5.3, questions 9, 12)

20. **Air (question 9).** Air is 10. The avatar has connected air when a path of open cells that are neither flooded nor gassy joins its cell to the surface, or to an active camp whose air line is intact (item 29). With connected air it refills fully in step (e); without, or in a flooded or gassy cell, it loses 1. At 0 the run ends: drowned in water, suffocated otherwise.
21. **Feeling the air.** Whether the avatar has connected air is shown to the player as a tell ("the air is fresh" or "the air is stale"), computed on the true cube like damp stone. It is the one thing shown that can depend on unrevealed cells, and it is not part of the forecast or the action list.
22. **Death causes.** Crushed (rockfall brings health to 0 or below), drowned, suffocated, burned (lava or an explosion brings health to 0 or below), fell, out of fuel, entombed. One scenario test for each.
23. **Entombment (question 12).** "Give up" is an action that ends the run as entombed. It is offered when a search finds no way out. The search uses revealed cells only: a move counts only if every cell it is judged by (decision note 0011) is revealed, and an unrevealed cell is never entered. It finds the fewest ladders needed to reach the surface (each step that needs a ladder placed costs one; chosen drops and steps up count as moves; clearing rubble and mining do not). "Give up" is offered when no route exists or the cheapest needs more ladders than the avatar carries plus 3 for each timber. It is a pure function of the state, worked out whenever the action list is built; nothing is stored. It errs toward offering, which is safe because it is never forced.
24. **How this differs from 5.3.** 5.3 describes entombment as having no path and lacking the fuel and materials to make one. Item 23 leaves fuel and mining out on purpose: a miner with a pick can nearly always cut steps, so "entombed" will mostly record a choice to stop, and a miner in a pit with no ladders can always end the run (issue #28). The random sim policy never chooses "give up", so that it cannot hide a soft-lock.

### Camps (section 8, [Decided]; what it leaves open is assumed)

25. **Camp kit.** Weighs 8; the avatar carries at most one, as section 8 says.
26. **Founding.** Costs the shift's remaining AP (at least 1). Judged by 27 cells centred on the avatar: the 18 camp cells (3×3, 2 high, the avatar in the lower layer) must be cleared, meaning open with no structure, water, lava, or gas; the 9 cells under them must be solid. Founding is offered only when all 27 are revealed.
27. **Functions.** In any camp cell: camp recipes (7.2), 1 AP each; put into and take from the stash (0 AP, no limit); rest, which costs the shift's remaining AP (at least 1; this reads 4.1's "whole shift" as "the rest of the shift", the looser reading) and in step (e) restores health to full, and air to full if the camp's air line is intact.
28. **Upkeep.** As design doc 4 gives it: each active camp burns 1 fuel every 4 shifts, counted from the shift it was founded, in addition to the avatar's own. Decommissioning (1 AP, in a camp cell) stops it, ends the camp's functions, and leaves the stash as loose items.
29. **Air line.** A camp's air line is intact when a path of open cells joins a camp cell to the surface in which every stretch that is flooded or gassy is at most 8 cells long and has a vent at each end. So vents exist to carry air across bad stretches; with no bad stretch any open path will do.
30. **Destruction.** A camp is destroyed when water or lava enters any of its cells, an explosion includes or is beside one, or a falling block passes through or rests in one. Its stash is lost. Gas alone does not destroy it but cuts its air line. This is the harshest number in the note and is for the M2 playtest round to judge.
31. **Camp items.** Smelting 2 raw ore + 1 coal makes 2 ingots of weight 1 each (half the weight, as 7.2 says). Drill: carried, weight 3; hard rock costs 2 AP. Scan: 2 AP and 1 scanner charge, as 4.1 says; reveals the 3×3×3 region whose centre is up to 3 steps from the avatar in a straight line. Pump (weight 3), vent (2), safety lamp (1): placed structures, 1 AP. Explosive, sealant, scanner charge: weight 1 each.

### Supply (questions 13, 53, 19)

32. **Timber (question 13).** Timber-root stays in layers 0–7; hauling timber down is the intended cost of supports, ladders, and camp kits.
33. **Starting fuel (question 53; owner: "Start with fuel to get to coal so we don't need to have close coal necessary").** Coal need not be near the start. Starting fuel must let a player reach coal and work it on any seed: a balance task measures, over at least 1,000 seeds, the shifts a sensible route needs to reach the nearest coal (surface or underground) and come back fuel-positive, and raises starting fuel in `src/data/loadouts.ts` if the worst seeds cannot. Coal density is not changed.
34. **Zero fuel (question 19; owner: "Zero fuel is a almost certain death sentence in a tunnel").** The rule stands: fuel is tested at upkeep before the shift's fuel is taken, so a miner gets one last shift at zero. The sentences in design doc 4 and 5.3 are reworded to say so.

### Code

35. **Versions.** `ENGINE_VERSION` becomes 0.3.0 in the first M2 PR (the skeleton task) and stays there until the M2 playtest round. `STATE_VERSION` rises with each change to the shape of the state.
36. **Sim.** The M2 acceptance run is 1,000 random-policy runs with no exception and no soft-lock; the sim reports death causes and, for each death, whether a tell or countdown for its cause was on screen beforehand.

## Consequences

- A straight ladder shaft, the cheapest descent in both M1 playtest rounds, now needs a support every few cells or it sheds its walls down its whole length: each fall destroys the ladders in the column and deals 4 damage to a miner anywhere in it, with a crack and a countdown two shifts ahead. Supports cost timber and stone, so stone has a use (question 43) and timber is contested (question 13). The M2 playtest brief asks first whether the descent has stopped being automatic.
- Item 10 is the largest addition to the design doc's rules in this note. It is for the owner to confirm or replace (question 56).
- Item 11 makes the dirt band fiddly: tunnels and stairs under dirt need supports or lose their roofs. It is the design doc's rule as written; whether it is fun is for the M2 playtest round.
- Items 12–16 choose simple flow rules that are deterministic. Balance comes in M6.

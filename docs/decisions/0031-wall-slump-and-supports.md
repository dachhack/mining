# 0031 — T8: pit walls slump, and supports

Date: 2026-11-20. Status: accepted for the code as it stands (issue #94, task T8 of decision note 0025). It builds design doc v0.2 section 6.1, which the owner chose ("Pit walls slump") and whose rule and numbers are [Default] (questions 81 and 82). The readings below are the developer's, made where 6.1 leaves a detail open. None changes a [Decided] item.

## Context

After T5 the cube phase had five steps, of which (a) to (d) were empty, and placing a support was a stub. Design doc 6.1 replaces v0.1's cave-ins, spans and shafts with one rule read off the heightmap.

## Decision

### The rules as built

1. **Slide path, foot and fall** are pure functions over a table of every square's depth (`DepthMap` in `src/engine/heightmap.ts`): `slidePathIn`, `footIn`, `fallIn`, with `slidePath`, `footOf` and `fallOf` for the cube as it stands. From a square the path steps to its lowest face neighbour (ties north, east, south, west) while that neighbour is deeper; the foot is where it stops.
2. **Unstable** (`src/engine/slump.ts`: `slumpCheck`, `unstableSquares`). A square is _steep_ if it is `HAZARDS.slump.steepStep` (2) or more above a face neighbour, and _tall_ if its fall is more than `slumpLimit` of its top block (`src/data/blocks.ts`: dirt and rubble 1, hard rock and crystal 3, every other block 2). A square with a tree, with a support, or with no block left is never unstable. Under a tree the block is not read at all.
3. **Cracks.** In step (a) of the cube phase every unstable square without a crack cracks, showing `HAZARDS.slump.crackCountdown` (2). In step (d) each running countdown goes down by 1; a crack made in the same phase is not ticked, so a fresh crack shows 2 through the whole next shift and falls at the end of the shift after that ("the shift after next", 6.1). A countdown runs only while its square is unstable.
4. **The slump.** In step (a), before the new cracks, every block whose countdown shows 1 and whose square is unstable at that moment slides to the foot of its slide path (worked out at that moment) and lies there as rubble. A support, pump, vent or explosive on either square is destroyed (cause `rockfall`), a camp on either is destroyed, and a stump on the square it leaves is gone for good, as when a square is mined.
5. **Cascades, and the fixed order.** Squares are always visited row by row (y, then x). The blocks that are due are listed first, then each is looked at in that order: one whose square an earlier slump made stable stays where it is, and so does a square whose due block an earlier slump buried: the rubble now on top has no crack, so it waits for its own (found in review of PR #98). A block falls at most once in a cube phase, and only a block that showed a crack at 1 before the phase began. New instability shows as new cracks, each with its own two-shift warning.
6. **Termination.** `validateData` requires every slump limit to be at least 1 and the steep step at least 2, so an unstable square's foot is always at least 2 deeper and a slumping block comes to rest at least one layer lower. `slumpBlock` throws rather than move a block sideways or up. A property test (40 random pits of random materials and far steeper than a player could dig) checks that every slump goes strictly down, nothing lands above layer 1, no block is made or lost, and the pit comes to rest.
7. **Supports.** `{ type: 'placeSupport', x, y, ap }` (the action named a cell before; it names a square now, like a mine). 1 AP and 1 support; any square, no reach. Refused with no support in the stockpile, on a tree, on a square with no block left, and on a square that already holds a structure (`supportCheck`). A support holds its own square only. Lava and camp squares are left to T10 and T12, as in `mine.ts`.
8. **Crew damage.** A slump records `CREW.damage.slump` (3) through `recordDamage` for the square the block leaves and the square it lands on; T5's mechanism does the rest (worked squares only, once per kind, "buried"). Placing a support marks its square as worked.
9. **The forecast** (`previewCubePhase`). `countdowns` lists every cracked square the player sees, with its number, whether it is running, and where the block would land. `slumps` is new: the events step (a) would emit, got by running the real step on `knownCube(state)`. So the preview cannot disagree with the rule.
10. **`STATE_VERSION` is 11** (T4 took 10; T7 and T9 kept it). The arrays are the same, but where a structure is stored changed (reading 1), so a version-10 save is refused. `ENGINE_VERSION` stays 0.4.0. The play-log format stays 3: no log ever held a `placeSupport`.
11. **Data.** `BlockDef.slumpLimit` replaces `span` and `shaftLimit`; `HAZARDS.slump` (`crackCountdown`, `steepStep`) replaces `HAZARDS.caveIn`. `cube/caveIns.ts` is now `cube/slumps.ts`.

### Readings where 6.1 is silent

1. **Where a support is.** A structure on a square is stored in the cell of the square's top block (`structureOnSquare`). So it belongs to that block: mining the block takes it away (`structureDestroyed`, cause `mined`; question 82), and rubble landing on the square buries it. This holds for any structure, so T11 and T13 get the same behaviour for explosives, pumps and vents.
2. **A crack on a square that becomes stable again stays, stopped** (design doc 6.1). Issue #94 said "the crack clears"; the coordinator settled it: follow the design doc, and show a stopped crack differently from a counting one (question 99). The choice is in two places only: step (a) skips a square that already has a crack, and step (d) ticks only unstable squares. A crack on a block that rubble buries is removed: that block is no longer a top block.
3. **A slump off the roof of an underground space** collapses the space exactly as a mine does: `slumpBlock` calls T9's `collapseSpaceUnder` (`src/engine/breach.ts`) for the cell the block left. The square goes down exactly 1 level, and what the space held stands on it. This replaces the temporary fill-with-rubble rule of note 0026, which the first draft of this task used.
4. **The warning before a mine never names the mined square,** and it never needs to: the level rule allows a mine only when no neighbour is higher, so the square can be unstable afterwards only if it already stood 2 above a neighbour, and then it was unstable before. Whether such a square is still unstable depends on the block the mine uncovers, which is not seen yet (question 101).
5. **The forecast takes an uncovered block to be plain rock**, as the flows forecast does (0014 item 4). If the block a slump uncovers is weaker, that square may crack as well. This is the one way the slump forecast can fall short, and it has its own test.
6. **"A mine of a square whose crack falls at the end of this shift"** (design doc 11) is not treated as a risky tap here: mining the cracked block removes it. T14 decides whether that tap should be confirmed.
7. **Contents under landed rubble** (question 100). Water, lava and gas standing on a square are stored in the open cells above its top block, usually in the lowest one (note 0030), which is the cell a slumping block lands in. Left alone they would sit inside the rubble: still in the state, never shown, and out of reach of every later rule. So `liftContents` moves whatever that cell holds to the cell above before the rubble is placed: the contents rise with the square, in the same amounts, and a lava source stays a source. The cell above always exists and is open, because rubble lands at least one layer below ground level.
8. **A space opened from the side stays sealed** (question 102). Lowering a square, by a slump or by a mine, can put open air beside a cell of a cavern or pocket that lies under a higher neighbour. `collapseSpaceUnder` looks only under the cell a block left, and this task adds nothing: a space is breached only when its roof goes. A slump therefore behaves exactly as a mine does, and nothing new can pour out of a wall.

### Tools

- **Play tool.** `support X,Y` places a support. A mine that would make squares unstable is not taken the first time: the refusal is the warning, naming each square and why, and `mine! X,Y` takes it. The tool replays its log for every command and keeps nothing between them, so "type it again" could not work; the "!" is the second tap. Notes show a crack and its countdown; the Hazards line says when each cracked block falls and where it lands; "If you end the shift now" says which blocks slump and which squares crack. The rules text has a "Pit walls slump" section.
- **Phone preview.** A crack is a red zigzag with its countdown; a support is the T mark. A "Place support (n)" toggle over the map is the first tool of the tool bar: on, a tap places a support. A mine that would make squares unstable needs a second tap on the same square; the first marks those squares in orange and says why. T14 polishes all of it.
- **Simulator.** The `pit` policy now takes only mines that make no square unstable, and when a square is unstable all the same it mines it down, or supports it if it cannot. A new `greedy` policy is the old pit policy: it ignores slumps and is buried within about 60 decisions on every seed tried.
- **Golden runs** are re-recorded: see `tests/golden/README.md`.

## Consequences

- **Pits are shallower.** The `pit` policy ends every run alive but reaches depth 3 to 5 (mean about 3.4); before slumps it reached 4 to 7. The level benches dirt needs roughly double the pit's width for each level. This bears on question 85 ("measure first"): T15's depth survey should measure with benches and with supports.
- **No scripted run sees mineral traces any more**, so the golden run `pit-seed-31-traces` is gone and two simulator tests now pin "no traces" where they pinned "traces seen". Checked again on the cubes of issue #95, which have caverns and pockets: `pit` on seeds 1 to 150, `greedy` on 1 to 30 and `random` on 1 to 40 see none. Traces keep their unit tests (`tests/engine/tells.test.ts`) and the hidden-information tests. A policy that reaches ore (T15) should bring a traces golden run back.
- T10 to T13 can rely on `structureOnSquare`, `topCellOf` and the `DepthMap` functions, and their hazards only need to leave the heights as they are: a step of 2 they make cracks and slumps by itself.

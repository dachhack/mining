# 0042 — UI lab 2: fog of war around camps, camps you place and upgrade

Date: 2026-11-21. Status: implemented (issue #118). No game rule, game data number or golden hash changes; `ENGINE_VERSION` stays 0.4.0, and nothing under `src/engine` changed.

## What

- **A third button on the start screen**, "Lab 2" (`#lab2`, or `#lab2/110` for seed 110). Lab 1 and the game are unchanged.
- **Lab 2 is Lab 1 plus camps** (`src/app/lab/campWorld.ts`). Same cube, blocks, shadows, tap-to-mine, level rule and counter, drawn by the same code.
- **Fog.** Each camp lights a circle of squares. Lit squares are drawn as in Lab 1 and can be mined. Squares lit once but not now are drawn grey and cannot be mined. The rest is dark fog: taps there do nothing at all. The edge of the lit area has a thin warm line, a soft shade inside it and a little light spilling into the dark.
- **Camps stand on a 3 × 3 patch of level ground** (owner, question 134). The patch has a warm wash and a gold edge, with a tent marker (and the level in a tag) on its middle square; none of the nine squares can be mined while the camp stands. A tap on any of them opens the camp's panel: level, ring, what it mines, what the next level gives, the upgrade's cost (what you have of each item, out of what it costs), an "Upgrade" button and "Demolish". The camp tool ("New camp", or a long press on a lit square) asks for the middle of the patch, then shows the 3 × 3 (gold when a camp can stand there; red and dashed when not, with the squares at another depth washed red and "Ground not level: a camp needs a flat 3 × 3"), the new ring (dashed), the squares it would light, the cost, and a "Place camp" button.
- **Mining ability** of a square is the best level among the camps whose ring covers it. A block too hard for that level is refused with its own cue: a blue padlock, "Needs Lv 2" floating up, and a blue hint ("Hard rock needs a level 2 camp over it"). The too-steep refusal keeps Lab 1's yellow cue and shake.

## Choices

- **A circle, not face steps.** A diamond of face steps looks like a rotated square on a square grid; a circle reads as "the reach of a lantern". `inCircle` counts a square when dx² + dy² ≤ r² + r (a circle of about r + ½), which leaves no single squares poking out at the four points. Radius 3 lights 37 squares, 7 across.
- **Starting numbers** (all in `src/data/labCamps.ts`, which the game never reads). The owner widened the rings after the first draft ("a wider radius to make sure resources are obtainable"): 5 / 8 / 12, in place of 3 / 5 / 8.

  | Level | Ring | Adds                                                              | Cost to reach it                       |
  | ----- | ---- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
  | 1     | 5    | dirt, rubble, stone, coal seam, timber-root                       | new camp: 10 stone (the first is free) |
  | 2     | 8    | hard rock                                                         | 25 stone                               |
  | 3     | 12   | iron ore, copper ore, crystal (and unstable ore, relic, keystone) | 150 stone, 20 timber, 20 coal          |
  - **The first ring pays for the next two steps, in every seed.** With single-square camps, mining everything in the free camp's ring (radius 5) gave at least 63 stone in each of 200 seeds (median 83), but no coal in 40 of them and no timber in 26, so the second camp and the first upgrade cost stone only. A 3 × 3 camp pins more ground, and the ring then gives at least 37 stone (median 55), so the costs came down from 12 + 30 to 10 + 25 = 35. A unit test checks that all 200 seeds can pay for both from the first ring alone.
  - **The free camp** goes on the 3 × 3 patch with the most dirt on top (open ground) among those centred within 4 squares of the middle (`LAB_START_REACH`), the nearest of them winning a tie. A new world is level everywhere. In 200 seeds the patch has a median of 9 dirt squares out of 9 (never fewer than 7), and is at most 3.8 squares from the middle.
  - **The ring is measured from the camp's middle square**, as before; the 3 × 3 is always inside it.
  - **Level 3 needs more camps.** With single-square camps, a level-2 first camp alone collected a median of 287 stone, 11 timber and 14 coal (40 seeds), so 20 timber and 20 coal took another camp or two. Level 3's cost is unchanged by the 3 × 3 camps and was not surveyed again; see the next point for why ore is now harder to reach.
  - **Taps.** On seed 110, the second camp takes about 45 taps.

- **The camp's base pins the ground around it.** A camp's own squares are never mined, so they stay at the depth they were placed at, and the level rule then lets no square be deeper than its number of face steps from the base. Squares outside every ring stay level too, so a lone camp pins its middle at 0 and the rim at 0, and the deepest square is about half the ring's radius down: a lone level-2 camp (ring 8) digs to depth 5 at most (review of PR #119). Hard rock begins at layer 4, so the hard rock a level-1 camp uncovers is 4 steps from it and can never be mined while that camp stands, whatever its level; hard rock 5 or more steps out inside a level-2 ring can be (1 to 8 blocks per seed). The owner's answer (question 134): camps are 3 × 3 on level ground, and a camp can be demolished once a second camp covers its whole base, after which its ground can be mined ("dig under your old base"). With 3 × 3 bases more ground is pinned, so deep blocks need several overlapping level-2 rings: the e2e plan (`planUpgrade` in `tests/e2e/campPlan.ts`) finds ore that only level 3 unlocks next to the first camp in 2 of 10 seeds tried, after leapfrogging about 18 camps and upgrading four or five of them. So that an upgrade never breaks a promise, a tap checks the level rule before the hardness gate: a block that is too steep says "too steep", not "needs a level 2 camp".
- **Demolish** (replacing "Pack up", owner question 134): allowed only when every square of the camp's 3 × 3 is inside the ring of at least one other camp; otherwise the button stays, quieter, and a tap on it says "Build another camp that covers this one first." The camp goes and **gives nothing back** (as Pack up did; owner question 133). Its nine squares can then be mined, and what only it lit turns grey. Since another camp always covers it, the last camp can never be demolished.
- **The map never moves** (as Lab 1's review asked). Everything under the map is one tray of a fixed height (164 px); the key, the camp tool, its confirm bar and a camp's panel only swap what is inside it, each row one line high. An e2e test checks the map's and the tray's boxes after each panel opens and closes, and after taps.
- **Shared code with Lab 1.** Lab 1's screen was split into `labDraw.ts` (a square, its shadows, the tap cues), `labGestures.ts` (drag, pinch, wheel, tap, and an optional hold) and `labDom.ts` (title bar, counter, key). Lab 1 uses them unchanged in behaviour; its e2e tests pass as before.
- **A prepared world for the e2e tests.** `{ seed, steps }` in localStorage under `mining-game.lab2.prepared` opens Lab 2 with those steps replayed (taps, placings, upgrades, demolitions). The upgrade test uses it to start where a level-2 camp has dug into ore (seed 10, about 1,700 steps), rather than tapping them all. "New world" removes it; players never have one.
